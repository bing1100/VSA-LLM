## 10. Addendum 1 (2026-10-17, before any run of it): concatenation layouts and higher widths (LG-A, CPU)

**Origin:** the author, after the LG-A results: "we may need to use something like HRR cat to combine multiple to reduce
noise — also do these tests with higher dimensions — and with concatenation".
- HRRCat in the author's HRRBERT paper (`resources/vsa-paper.md`) concatenates an HRR part with a free part rather than
  superposing them.
- Here the same principle, concatenate instead of superpose, is tested on the logic store against one wide HRR of equal
  total width D.

**Layouts:**
- `wide`: one HRR of width D. Every binding is superposed (the tier-P layout).
- `cat-redundant`: k = 4 independent HRR codes of width D/4 carrying the same content; similarities averaged over blocks
  (an ensemble).
- `cat-role`: a fact is the concatenation [predicate | arg1 | arg2 (| arg3)] of raw fillers, one block per role (no
  binding). A rule is estimated as a block-circulant map, per-frequency least squares over the blocks.
- `cat-fact`: a frame is the concatenation of its 8 facts, one block per fact (each block a wide HRR of width D/8). A
  rule applies blockwise.
- `cat-slot`: one block per relation (the repository's slotted layout, decision 61c); `cat-slot8`: 8 groups of
  relations, balanced by expected load.
- `ensemble-3`: the three trained T4 C5 stores (seeds 1–3), cosine scores averaged (concatenated stores).

**Experiments:**
- **X1 rules:**
  - layouts `wide`, `cat-redundant`, `cat-role`; frames `wide` against `cat-fact`;
  - D ∈ {1,024, 4,096, 8,192, 16,384}; rule kinds as A1; k ∈ {3, 10, 30};
  - trained T5 rows in `cat-role` (native 256 per block) against `wide` 256.
- **X2 chains:**
  - layouts `wide`, `cat-redundant`, `cat-slot`, `cat-slot8`;
  - D ∈ {1,024, 4,096, 8,192, 16,384}; load ∈ {16, 32, 64}; fixed and heavy-tailed loads;
  - two queries: the single-filler chain relation (hops 1–8), and recall of a heavy relation that holds a quarter of the
    frame's edges.
- **X3 joins:** A3 with `wide` against `cat-slot` (8 base relations), D ∈ {256, 1,024, 4,096}, loads 8 and 16.
- **X4–X6 higher widths:**
  - A4 nested decode to depth 8;
  - A7 nested-vector trees at D ∈ {4,096, 8,192, 16,384};
  - A5 negation as failure at loads 32 and 64;
  - A6 numbers at 100 and 1,000 levels;
  - A8 resonator at D ∈ {2,048, 4,096, 8,192}.
- **X7 trained ensemble:** A11's rules and per-slot decode on T4 held-out and seen entries, single store against
  `ensemble-3`.

**Predictions:**
- (X-P1) `cat-redundant` ≈ `wide` at equal total width (|difference| ≤ 0.03). Averaging k codes of width D/k has the
  noise of one code of width D, so redundancy buys nothing beyond width.
- (X-P2) `cat-role` makes the fixed-point rules expressible: symmetric and sub-relation ≥ 0.95 at D ≥ 4,096 with
  k ≥ 10, against ≤ 0.16 for `wide`.
- (X-P3) `cat-fact` frames: rule application on 8-fact frames ≥ 0.95 at D ≥ 8,192, against ≤ 0.02 for `wide`.
- (X-P4) Chains:
  - `cat-slot` keeps the single-filler chain relation at ≥ 0.99 at every load for D ≥ 4,096;
  - it is worse than `wide` for the heavy relation at equal total width (isolation helps light relations and hurts heavy
    ones).
- (X-P5) Width:
  - `wide` chains at load 64 reach ≥ 0.95 at D = 16,384;
  - nested-vector trees still decay with depth (≤ 0.8 at depth 8 even at 16,384).
- (X-P6) `ensemble-3` raises T4 decode accuracy over the mean single store. Exploratory, no size predicted.

All are descriptive CPU experiments, with no Holm family. The predictions are read as stated.

## 11. Addendum 2 (2026-10-17, before any run of it): multi-round active reading with relation discovery (RD-11)

**Origin:** the author:
> "for reading — we may need to read multiple times — plus do some kind of reasoning search — reflecting on the
> relations that are available … the gold may not be possible without the relationships existing — this is an
> iterative process where even an iterative increase is good … multiple rounds of reading to condense and refine the
> knowledge learned … passive learning (gradient descent) — active learning reading — passive learning — active
> reading …"

- **Tier P (here) covers the active reading rounds.** The interleaved passive gradient rounds are tier F (plan RD-12).
- **Prior evidence:** E10.9a (synthetic) paired passive learning with self-reflection on the model's own vectors and was
  not faster than passive alone.
- **What differs here:** the active step re-reads external text and may create relations.

**Design (T4 held-out, 200 ChEBI definitions; T1 MeSH scope notes, 150):**
- **Round 0:** the reader (Claude, `claude -p`) starts with the track's relations, minus, on T4, four **hidden**
  relations: `has_functional_parent`, `is_conjugate_acid_of`, `is_conjugate_base_of`, `has_part`.
- **Each round r = 0, 1, 2:**
  - **Read twice** (two passes; relation order shuffled differently). Each fact is a partner copied from the text plus a
    listed relation, or `NEW` with a proposed name and description when no listed relation fits.
  - **Consolidate:** facts proposed in both passes (same relation, same resolved partner atom) are kept.
  - **Reflect:** one call clusters the round's `NEW` proposals into at most 6 relation types (name, description,
    members) and flags proposals that duplicate an existing relation. A type with at least 8 supporting proposals is
    accepted and listed, with its description, from the next round on.
- **Measured per round:**
  - (i) edges per definition, single pass against consolidated;
  - (ii) precision of visible-relation edges against gold;
  - (iii) **gold recall over all 13 T4 relations**. Each accepted new relation is mapped to the hidden gold relation of
    best edge Jaccard over the read terms, if that Jaccard is ≥ 0.2;
  - (iv) the Jaccard of each hidden relation's best match;
  - (v) the reachable ceiling (gold edges whose partner the text names);
  - (vi) the share of proposals that are `NEW`, and of partners the dictionary cannot resolve.
  - On T1, (i), (vi) and the accepted relation types are descriptive.

**Predictions:**
- (a) T4 gold recall over all relations rises from round 0 to round 2. Each later round is at least as good as the one
  before, within 0.01.
- (b) At least 2 of the 4 hidden relations are rediscovered (Jaccard ≥ 0.3) by round 2.
- (c) Consolidation raises visible-relation precision over a single pass by ≥ 0.05.
- (d) On T1, the `NEW` share falls across rounds.

**Budget:** `--max-usd 15` for both tracks, within the tier-P teacher cap of $25 ($4.15 spent). Open-licence text only
(decision 58).
- **2026-10-17, before the RD-11 runs: sizes reduced to fit the cap.** A labelled smoke (8 T4 definitions, 2 rounds) cost
  $0.32, about $0.065 per call. Runs: T4 160 definitions and T1 120, 10 per call, 3 rounds; `--max-usd` 8 (T4) and 7
  (T1). The design, measures and predictions are unchanged.
- **2026-10-17, before it runs: X8, tree layouts by the tree's structure** (author: "use an algorithm on how to
  concatenate the vector based on the tree structure").
  - **Trees:** complete binary trees, depth ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12}; 20 features × 8 values; 10 classes; 5 trees × 100
    inputs per depth; 3 seeds.
  - **Layouts:**
    - (a) one nested vector, D ∈ {4,096, 16,384};
    - (b) node memory (associative addresses, clean-up per step), block b ∈ {64, 256};
    - (c) **heap concatenation**: node i in block i, children 2i+1 and 2i+2 (positional; no address clean-up), b ∈ {64, 256};
    - (d) **level concatenation**: one block of width L ∈ {1,024, 4,096} per depth, the level's nodes superposed under
      position keys.
  - **Reported:** accuracy and total width.
  - **Predictions:**
    - (c) ≥ 0.99 at every depth with b = 256;
    - (b) degrades at large depth when b is small, from address collisions among 2^d nodes;
    - (d) degrades with depth once 2^depth approaches L / 8;
    - (a) as X4.
  - **Smoke fix:** the input record is encoded at a fixed 4,096 for every layout. At block width it confounded record
    reading with tree storage.

## 12. Addendum 3 (2026-10-17, before any run of it): RD-12, the interleaved passive ↔ active reading cycle (pilot)

**Origin:** the author's go-ahead ("please go ahead") for the passive → active reading → passive → … cycle (proposal
§9.6; plan RD-12).

**Pilot scope:** one track, one model, one seed.

**Design:**
- **Model:** T4, SmolLM2-360M C5 s1, continued from its final checkpoint (`train.init_mode: continue`; a grown relation
  count keeps the trained rows).
- **Read terms:** RD-11's 160 held-out T4 definitions (same selection).
- **Rounds:** 3, each a passive phase then evaluation.
- **Passive phase:** 2M tokens of continued training on a fixed mix, shared by every arm:
  - the 160 definitions, repeated (10% of tokens);
  - T4 training-domain abstracts (90%), which never name a held-out term.
  - The read terms are linked (full T4 alias table) and composed from the arm's frames.

**Arms (the frames of the 160 read terms in round r):**

| Arm | Frames |
|---|---|
| `cycle` | RD-11's active step on the current relation set (read twice → consolidate → reflect), written as frames, new relations as new relation ids. **Feedback:** after passive round r, each discovered relation is retracted from the relation set if removing its edges lowers the loss after the read terms by > 0.5% (relative). Reflection adds new proposals. |
| `single` | round-0 consolidated facts with the initial relations only, every round (one reading, no discovery) |
| `gold` | the gold frames (the knowledge ceiling) |
| `passive` | no frames for the read terms (passive learning only; matched compute) |

**Measures after each round's passive phase:**
- (i) mean token loss after the read terms in T4's evaluation windows;
- (ii) E11 items for the 160 terms (property, paraphrase, entailment):
  - implicit (frames composed);
  - explicit (the arm's frames written as recall lines before each item);
- (iii) general loss (forgetting) on 64 general windows;
- (iv) the arm's gold recall;
- (v) retractions.

**Primary** (one seed; pilot):
- `cycle − passive` and `cycle − single` on (i) at round 3, by a window bootstrap over terms.
- Predictions:
  - `cycle` < `passive` (lower loss after the read terms);
  - `cycle` ≤ `single`;
  - `gold` lowest.
- **Secondary:** explicit-recall property accuracy, `cycle − passive` > 0.

**Cost and placement:**
- About 1.5 GPU-h per arm (≈ 6 GPU-h); teacher ≤ $6 for `cycle` rounds 1–2 (round 0 is RD-11's cache).
- Queued after tier P (54.495996; report 54.4959969). Tier F scales seeds, the 135M model and T7-ROOD.
- **2026-10-17, smoke fix (before the RD-12 runs):** in evaluation, read terms with an empty frame get no row
  (`channel.skip_empty_frames`, E11's "none"). The `passive` arm needs this.
- **2026-10-20, run fix (after the first attempt, before any RD-12 result is read):** the passive phase sets
  `channel.skip_empty_frames` as evaluation does. Without it, the `cycle`, `single` and `passive` arms crashed in
  round 0's training (`every composed concept needs at least one edge`) as soon as a read term without a frame was linked.
  The `gold` arm had a frame for every read term, so the change cannot touch its run; it is not repeated. Round 0's
  teacher reads came from RD-11's cache ($0). The design is unchanged.
- **2026-10-20, second run fix (before any RD-12 result is read):** the retry failed because each failed round's
  model folder still held a config file, and the trainer refuses a non-empty folder. The passive phase now empties
  the folder first; it holds only that round's weights, which are recomputed on every attempt. The design is
  unchanged.

## 13. Addendum 4a (2026-10-20, before any run of it): RD-4b, selective recall and a context-free null (tier F block)

**Origin:** tier P's RD-4 result and diagnostics (`manuscript/findings/2026-10-20-e14-tier-p-gpu.md` §1). The author's
instruction of 2026-10-20, "Fix all of these and queue up failed experiments", given in reply to the three proposed
follow-ups, is the go-ahead for this block and for §§14–15.

**What tier P left open:**
- On T4, explicit recall of the gold frame lowered property accuracy (−0.10), although the recall named the gold answer for
  399 of 407 items.
- Two explanations were seen after the primaries (exploratory):
  - the PMI null prompt read the same recall context;
  - a 15-line list of real ChEBI names dilutes what the 360M model already knows.
- RD-4b separates the two. Tier P's pre-registered result stands as recorded; RD-4b does not replace it.

**Items:** as tier P §1 (`t4-heldout-smollm2-v1`, `t5-new-smollm2-v1`; property, paraphrase, entailment).

**Scoring:**
- **The null prompt is read without any context** (`--null plain`): the template with the null surface and the
  candidate, computed once per run. PMI = log p(candidate | context + item prompt) − that null.
- Raw (uncalibrated) choices are also recorded.

**Conditions** (`--family rd4b`):
- `none`;
- `explicit-sym:oracle` (tier P's whole-frame recall, now with the plain null);
- **`explicit-rel:<reader>`** for oracle, stated, typeprior and linker: before each item, only the reader's edges of
  **that item's relation**, as recall lines;
- **`explicit-rel-wrong:oracle`:** the same lines with every partner replaced by another filler of that relation. This
  is the content control;
- `definition`;
- `implicit:oracle` (compose hosts).

**Hosts:**
- SmolLM2-360M C5 on T4 and T5, seeds 1–3;
- C0′ s1 on both tracks (explicit conditions only; C5 s1's frames, as in tier P).

**Primary** (per track, C5 hosts; Holm over 3 contrasts × 3 tests):
- (a) `explicit-rel:oracle − none`;
- (b) `explicit-rel:oracle − explicit-sym:oracle`;
- (c) `explicit-rel:oracle − implicit:oracle`.

**Secondary:**
- `explicit-sym:oracle − none` with the plain null;
- `explicit-rel-wrong:oracle − none` (predicted negative);
- `explicit-rel:oracle − explicit-rel-wrong:oracle`;
- `explicit-rel:<reader> − none` for stated, typeprior and linker;
- `definition − explicit-rel:oracle` and `definition − none`;
- the primary contrasts on raw choices.

**Stated-only items (RD-4b proper; descriptive, with intervals):**
- The items whose gold answer is in the `stated` reader's frame. In the smoke run that was about 5% of T4's property
  items, roughly 20 per seed, too few for a confirmatory test.
- Contrasts: `explicit-rel:stated − none`, `definition − none` and `explicit-rel:stated − definition`.

**Predictions:**
- T4: (a), (b) and (c) are positive on property.
- T5: (a) and (c) are positive. (b) is about 0, since T5 frames are short.
- `explicit-rel-wrong:oracle` is below `none` on both tracks.
- With the plain null, `explicit-sym:oracle − none` on T4 is above tier P's −0.10.

**Decision rule:**
- **If (a) and (c) are positive (Holm) on T4 property,** the recommended self-learning route becomes read → store →
  **selective** explicit recall (the item's relation lines). An E13 amendment is then drafted for the author. It is not
  queued.

## 14. Addendum 4b (2026-10-20, before any run of it): LG-B with conclusions written as verdicts

  Y";

This block tests whether the failure is only the wording.

**Items** (`e14_items verdict`):
- `taxonomy-verdict` (1,200 true/false items of `taxonomy-s1`) and `rulebase-verdict` (600 items of `rulebase-s1`);

  - "Conclusion: X is not a Y. (0.74)" becomes "Conclusion: the claim "X is a Y" is False. (0.74)";
  - a positive conclusion becomes "… is True.";
  - no conclusion becomes "… is unknown.";
