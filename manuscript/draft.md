## Abstract

*(Skeleton; see [outline.md](outline.md) for the slot list.)*

Byte-pair tokenizers optimize compression, not meaning. A language model must therefore learn, from data and parameters alone, how the fragments of a multi-token word compose into a concept. We call this cost the *syntax → semantics tax*. We ask whether a curated ontology can pay part of it, through a span channel added to a causal LM.

In the channel, each linked concept's vector is composed from its ontology relation–filler edges by binding, with no per-concept parameters† ("by fixed binding"). A causal context query can re-weight the edges; as its temperature grows it reduces to the static HRR bundle of HRRBERT. The shared dictionary of atomic and relation vectors can grow through a first-order, usage-level split test read from optimizer state. The composed vector is added at the last subtoken of a prefix-causally linked span.

On planted structure each mechanism is identifiable:
- the context query recovers context-dependent compositions the static bundle cannot (+0.36 to +0.46 held-out cosine);
- ontology-induced edge weights transfer to composition-disjoint concepts (0.976 vs 0.60–0.70 for free factors);
- the split test recovers every planted polysemous atomic and relation sub-type, with ≤ 1% surviving false splits.

On frozen GPT-2 anchors for WordNet and MeSH, relation labels carry signal, but no binding operator separates from a random fixed or untyped one.

`[[R2: E1 and E3 in one sentence each]]` In 50M and 125M causal LMs trained from scratch on a FineWeb-Edu × WordNet stream, `[[R3: held-out and seen-rare stratified loss vs free per-concept table, random span vectors, shuffled frames and hashed n-gram memory; locality; probes; data multiplier k(L)]]`. `[[R3: operator ablation; framing as compositional parameter sharing unless binding separates from random_fixed]]` `[[R4–R7: pretrained hosts, quantization, explanations (LLM-graded estimate), self-authored ontologies, application tracks]]` All experiments fit about 400 GPU-hours on one RTX 3090.

## 1. Introduction

**The tax.** A BPE tokenizer is fitted to compress text. The segmentation it learns is good for compression and indifferent to meaning. Every token then gets a free embedding row, and the transformer must learn, from co-occurrence alone, that the three pieces of a rare multi-token word name one concept, what kind of thing that concept is, and how it relates to the concepts around it. Mechanistic work shows how much effort this takes. LMs spend early-layer computation assembling multi-token words at their last subtoken and "erasing" subtoken identity (Feucht et al. 2024; Kaplan et al. 2025). Dedicated induction heads read lexical units at word ends (Feucht et al. 2025). Hashed n-gram memories that relieve the early layers of this reconstruction improve iso-parameter models (Huang et al. 2025; Cheng et al. 2026).

We call the data and parameters spent on the step from multi-token surface form to concept the **syntax → semantics tax**. We expect it to be largest where data and parameters are scarce: for rare, multi-token concepts and for small models. This is not the fertility-based "token tax" of multilingual tokenization (Lundin et al. 2025). We measure it as stratified loss on ontology-linked spans.

**Why an ontology, and why joint training.** For many domains a curated ontology already states what the multi-token words mean, as typed relations to other concepts:
- WordNet for general English;
- MeSH, SNOMED CT and ChEBI for biomedicine and chemistry;
- product taxonomies, EuroVoc and API schemas elsewhere.

Vector-symbolic architectures (VSAs) offer a way to turn such a frame into a vector. Bind each relation vector with its filler's atomic vector, then bundle the bound edges (Plate 1995; Gayler 2003; Kanerva 2009). HRRBERT used exactly this to build SNOMED CT code embeddings for a BERT over sequences of diagnosis codes. It trained the atomic vectors jointly with the transformer and reported gains on rare and unseen codes (Hu et al. 2024).

Our own first attempt went the other way: fit such compositions to the embedding rows of a *frozen* pretrained model and insert new rows. It failed. After an audit of our evaluation code (split leakage, test-set selection, metrics inflated by construction; Appendix A), circular-convolution composition showed no advantage over identity or a capacity-fair diagonal operator for one-hop transfer in frozen GPT-2 space. The recorded "best" low-rank transfer turned out to be a leakage artefact.

The only regime in which VSA composition has shown a behavioural benefit is HRRBERT's joint regime. That paper did not test data or parameter efficiency, small models, quantization or causal text LMs. We therefore move to the joint regime, on small causal LMs trained from scratch, and to continued pretraining of small pretrained hosts.

**What we claim, and how we frame it.** We do not presume that the binding operator matters. A bundle of bound edges is, at minimum, a form of **compositional parameter sharing**: concepts that share fillers and relations share parameters, so a rare concept's row is trained by the frequent concepts it overlaps with. Whether the *algebra* of binding adds anything beyond that sharing is an empirical question. We answer it with an operator ablation in every experiment (circular convolution, diagonal, low-rank, orthogonal, a random *fixed* operator, and no operator at all). The paper is framed as compositional parameter sharing over ontology edges unless the ablation separates a binding operator from the random fixed one `[[R3: outcome of C8]]`.

The same caution applies to the channel as a whole. Any gain could come from knowing *where* a multi-token concept ends (span-boundary information) or from extra lookup capacity rather than from ontology content. Every comparison is therefore made at matched non-embedding parameters and tokens against:
- random fixed span vectors;
- a free per-concept span table;
- the same composition machinery with *shuffled* frames;
- a hashed n-gram span memory;
- a relation-type-only composition.

**Contributions.**

1. **A compositional, growable span channel for causal LMs** whose concept vectors are built from ontology relation–filler edges by fixed binding, with no per-concept parameters. The vector is added in place at the last subtoken of a prefix-causally linked span during LM training† (§3.4). Linking runs on character offsets, so one linked corpus serves every tokenizer. A causal-leak property test guarantees that the input at position *t* depends only on tokens ≤ *t*.
2. **Three mechanisms on top of the static HRRBERT bundle (M0), each reducing exactly to the previous one when switched off:**
   - a query-conditioned weighting of VSA-bound ontology edges with zero per-concept parameters that recovers the static bundle as τ → ∞ (M1; §3.1);
   - an ontology-induced, transferable rank-*k* edge weighting (M2; §3.2);
   - a first-order, usage-level split test for shared dictionary vectors, read from optimizer state and calibrated by a permutation null (M3; §3.3).
3. **Identifiability on planted structure (E0; all gates pass)**, and a negative result on frozen anchors: on WordNet and MeSH, no operator separates from a random fixed or untyped one, although relation labels carry signal (E2).
4. **A pre-registered, stratified test of the tax** in 50M/125M causal LMs: held-out vs seen-rare vs frequent concepts, inside vs after spans, by span length, plus a data multiplier `k(L)` with fitted curves and projections. It runs against the five controls above `[[R3]]`.
5. `[[R4–R7: pretrained hosts and quantization; explanations and zero-shot rows; self-authored ontologies (M5); six application tracks]]`.
6. **Reproducibility and feasibility artefacts:**
   - bit-reproducible CPU results across two machines;
   - span-cardinality tables for six corpora × three tokenizers, which decide in advance where a span channel can be measured at all;
   - a sparse generation path whose memory does not grow with the ontology size.

## 2. Related work

*Condensed from the program's verified literature note (related-work.md, 2026-09-30) and the 2026-10 re-check. Venues marked ‡ are commonly cited and were not re-checked against proceedings.*

**Knowledge-injected LMs and compositional entity rows.**
- *Per-entity tables at mention spans:*
  - ERNIE fuses pretrained TransE entity vectors into BERT at aligned mentions (Zhang et al. 2019).
  - KnowBERT attends from a mention span over candidate entity embeddings, including WordNet synsets, and adds the result back into the residual stream (Peters et al. 2019).
  - LUKE treats entities as extra tokens (Yamada et al. 2020).
  - KALM gives a causal GPT-2 entity-aware input and an entity-prediction loss (Rosset et al. 2020). It is the closest analogue of our injection plus semantic head, but with a free per-entity table.
  - UmlsBERT adds a learned semantic-group embedding to UMLS-linked words, like a position embedding (Michalopoulos et al. 2021).
- *Composed rows that need no per-entity parameters:*
  - Bootleg composes types and relations for tail entities in disambiguation (Orr et al. 2021).
  - NodePiece tokenizes KG entities into anchors and relations (Galkin et al. 2022).
  - GRAM attends over a medical code's ancestors (Choi et al. 2017‡).
  - KEPLER encodes entity descriptions (Wang et al. 2021‡).
  - MKGL builds inductive KG-entity tokens for an LLM by neighbourhood aggregation over the LLM's own token embeddings (Guo et al. 2024).
  - HRRBERT binds SNOMED CT relations to filler atomic vectors with circular convolution (Hu et al. 2024).
- *Closest to our channel:* ConceptFormer (Barmettler et al. 2025). A generator trained with a frozen GPT-2 turns a Wikidata node's neighbours and edges into concept vectors presented to the LM as extra input positions.

We differ from these in combining four things:
- rows built from *bound* relation–filler edges of a curated ontology by a fixed operator, with the operator ablated;
- in-place injection into a causal LM trained on running text, from scratch or by continued pretraining;
- a leakage-safe, prefix-causal last-subtoken rule;
- matched-parameter comparisons against a free per-concept table, random and shuffled rows, a hashed memory and a type-only composition.

We do not claim the first compositional or zero-parameter entity rows.

**Context-conditioned composition; attention and binding.** Reading attention as soft unbinding is known:
- tensor product representations (Smolensky 1990) and the TP-Transformer (Schlag et al. 2019);
- linear attention as fast-weight outer-product binding (Schlag et al. 2021‡; Ba et al. 2016);
- Hopfield layers (Ramsauer et al. 2021‡);
- attention recast with HRR (Hrrformer; Alam et al. 2023), and non-commutative GHRR binding that "can implement a kind of attention" (Yeung et al. 2024).

Context-dependent weighting of lexical-knowledge components was shown in 2017:
- distributions over WordNet synsets and hypernyms per token (Dasigi et al. 2017);
- context attention over sememe-composed senses (Niu et al. 2017).

CokeBERT selects KG neighbour edges for a mentioned entity by softmax attention conditioned on the input text (Su et al. 2021). Its edge values are TransE-style translations of pretrained per-entity vectors, and its ablation against mean pooling plays the role of our τ → ∞ limit. Question-aware KG prompting conditions GNN aggregation on the question (Liu et al. 2025).

What we add is narrower. Edge values are role–filler *bindings* with no per-concept parameters. The query is built causally from preceding tokens. The τ → ∞ limit is exactly the static HRR bundle of HRRBERT, so training departs from the VSA prior instead of replacing it. We present the bundling–unbinding–attention correspondence as design motivation, not as a contribution.

**Factorized and compositional tables.**
- *Factorization and hashing:* low-rank adaptation (Hu et al. 2022‡), ALBERT's factorized embedding (Lan et al. 2020‡), quotient–remainder and tensor-train tables (Shi et al. 2020‡; Yin et al. 2021), and hashing (Weinberger et al. 2009; Svenstrup et al. 2017‡).
- *Large savings from shared sub-tables:* NodePiece, DHE (Kang et al. 2021), T-FREE (Deiseroth et al. 2024).

Our factored mapping (M2) is new only in its *ontology-supported* sparsity pattern with a concept factor induced from the frame, which transfers to unseen nodes. Any compression claim is made only against equal-bytes factorized, hashed, tensor-train and post-training-quantized tables.

**Growing and splitting.**
- *Splitting units into offset copies:* splitting steepest descent (Liu et al. 2019) and its signed variant (Wu et al. 2020); Linde–Buzo–Gray codebook growth (Linde et al. 1980).
- *A statistical test along a principal direction:* G-means (Hamerly & Elkan 2003).
- *Measuring gradient conflict and gradient SNR:* PCGrad (Yu et al. 2020); GSNR (Liu et al. 2020).
- *Duplicating a shared parameter whose users' gradients conflict, and partitioning the users:* Parameter Differentiation does this at task level in multilingual NMT (Wang & Zhang 2022); Recon makes the most-conflicted layers task-specific (Shi et al. 2023); a recent recommender splits scalar parameters whose scenario gradients have cosine < −0.5 (Lin et al. 2026).
- *Multi-sense embeddings induced by context clustering:* NP-MSSG (Neelakantan et al. 2014) and AdaGram (Bartunov et al. 2016‡). Li & Jurafsky (2015‡) caution that such gains often vanish against capacity-matched baselines.

M3's contribution is the *test*. It scores a split by the cancellation gain of per-usage momenta along the top between-usage eigenvector, read from Adam's state, and accepts it only against a permutation null. Splits are therefore statistically licensed rather than threshold-triggered. We credit the ±ε offset and sign partition to splitting and LBG, and the test-along-a-direction idea to G-means.

**Multi-token words and lookup memories.** One remedy changes the tokenizer: superword tokens (Liu et al. 2025; Schmidt et al. 2025), byte-level and pooled models (Yu et al. 2023‡; Pagnoni et al. 2025; Nawrot et al. 2023), or tokenizer transfer (Minixhofer et al. 2024). Another adds lookup memory for multi-token units:
- hashed multi-gram input vocabularies (Huang et al. 2025);
- Engram's gated hashed n-gram memory in the residual stream (Cheng et al. 2026);
- contextualized n-gram embeddings (Yu et al. 2025);
- training-free subtoken-mean memories (Qu et al. 2026);
- factorized and grafted successors that beat Engram (Zhou et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2026; Cheng et al. 2026b).

These are exactly the "span boundary or lookup" explanations a structured channel must beat. Our controls include a hashed span memory at matched parameters, injection site and gate. `[[G2: whether an Engram-faithful variant C1h-E is added; otherwise the claim is qualified to matched site and gate]]`

**Zero-shot rows for new concepts.** Rows for unseen words can be generated from text evidence:
- definitions (Bahdanau et al. 2017);
- contexts: à la carte (Khodak et al. 2018); attentive mimicking and BERTRAM (Schick & Schütze 2019, 2020);
- few-shot examples: CoLLEGe (Teehan et al. 2024);
- or from KG structure alone, as in NodePiece, Bootleg and MKGL.

We claim only *structure-only* rows for unseen ontology nodes, with no definitions or contexts, in a causal LM on node- and alias-disjoint splits. Text-evidence generators are reported separately.

**Self-authored knowledge.**
- *LMs author KGs and ontologies:* COMET (Bosselut et al. 2019), Symbolic Knowledge Distillation (West et al. 2022), LLMs4OL (Babaei Giglou et al. 2023), OLLM (Lo et al. 2024).
- *Small corpora can be expanded into synthetic text for continued pretraining:* EntiGraph (Yang et al. 2025), Synthesize-on-Graph (Ma et al. 2025), and SPA, a strong simple baseline (Tang et al. 2026).
- *Models improve from their own study data or self-edits:* Active Reading (Lin et al. 2025), SEAL (Zweiger et al. 2025), BoLT (Ruan et al. 2025).
- *Ontology-guided self-training exists:* OntoTune (Liu et al. 2025b), Evontree (Tu et al. 2025).
- *Synthetic data can be scored by its first-order effect on held-out loss:* TracIn (Pruthi et al. 2020), LESS (Xia et al. 2024), In-Run Data Shapley (Wang et al. 2025), Montessori-Instruct (Li et al. 2025), Optimsyn (Fan et al. 2026).
- *Replacing real data with self-generated data collapses models; accumulating it does not:* Shumailov et al. 2024; Gerstgrasser et al. 2024; Kazdan et al. 2025.

None of these has the same small model author *typed frames* for its own poorly modelled spans, admit edges by a held-out-utility lower bound, and consume them through a non-textual compositional channel. That combination, against compute-matched, synthetic-text and unstructured-notes controls, is what E7 tests `[[R6]]`.

**VSA in neural models.**
- HRR has been trained end to end in deep networks (Ganesan et al. 2021).
- It has been used for efficient attention (Alam et al. 2023), for KG embedding (Nickel et al. 2016) and for KG path retrieval alongside LLMs (Liu et al. 2025c).
- It has been used to analyse GPT-2 (Knittel et al. 2024).
- HRRBERT composed ontology embeddings for an encoder over code sequences.

We extend that line to causal text LMs, with the operator as an ablation.

## 3. Method

### 3.0 Notation and the static bundle (M0)

- $C$: the concept set (ontology nodes).
- $A$: the dictionary of atomic vectors.
- $R$: the dictionary of relation vectors; $a_k, r_k \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
- $F_i = \{(r_e, a_e)\}$: the frame of concept $i$, of degree $|F_i|$.
- $T_r$: a relation transform with adjoint $T_r^\top$; $v_e = T_{r_e}(a_e)$ is the bound edge.
- $N(x) = x/\lVert x\rVert$.
- $P$: a projector from the composer width $d$ to the model width $d_m$.

HRRBERT's concept vector (Hu et al. 2024, eq. 2) is the static bundle

$$c_i = N\Big(\sum_{e\in F_i} v_e\Big), \qquad v_e = r_e \circledast a_e .$$

We carry two fixes from the audit of the original implementation. Rows are normalized, not columns. Accumulation uses segment sums (`index_add_`), which do not drop duplicate destinations.

**Operator ladder.** The transform $T_r$ is an ablation axis, not a premise:

| Family | $T_r(x)$ | Parameters per relation |
|---|---|---|
| `hrr` | $r \circledast x$ (circulant) | $d$ |
| `hrr_identity` | as `hrr`, initialized at the identity spectrum | $d$ |
| `diagonal` | $r \odot x$ | $d$ |
| `low_rank` / `low_rank_tied` | $x + L_r R_r x$ (rank 1 in E2, parameter-matched to `hrr`) | $2dk$ |
| `orthogonal` | $Q_r x$ | $d^2$ |
| `random_fixed` | any of the above, frozen at initialization | 0 trainable |
| `untyped` | $x$ | 0 |

### 3.1 M1 — attentive composition with a context query

A concept's frame is a small key–value memory. Values are the bound edges; keys combine the edge's relation and filler:

$$k_e = W_K^r r_e + W_K^a a_e,\qquad u_i(q) = W_Q\,\bar c_i + W_C\,q,\qquad \bar c_i=\sum_{e\in F_i} v_e,$$
$$w_{i,e}(q) = |F_i|\;\mathrm{softmax}_{e\in F_i}\!\Big(\tfrac{\langle u_i(q),\,k_e\rangle}{\tau\sqrt{d_k}}\Big),\qquad c_i(q) = N\Big(\sum_{e\in F_i} w_{i,e}(q)\,v_e\Big).$$

The query is the concept's own bundle plus a projection of the context, so there are **no per-concept parameters** and the mapping applies unchanged to a concept never seen in training.

- **Exact reduction.** As $\tau\to\infty$, $w\to 1$ and $c_i(q)\to c_i$, the HRRBERT bundle. With near-orthogonal random atomics the weights also start near uniform at finite $\tau$. Training thus departs from the VSA prior instead of replacing it (property-tested to 1e-6).
- **Readout.** Querying with a relation probe instead of a context selects edges of that type. Unbinding their weighted sum with $T_r^\top$ and cleaning up against $A$ reads out the filler.
- **Explanation.** $w_{i,\cdot}(q)$ is a distribution over ontology edges for this occurrence.
- **Polysemy.** For a polysemous surface string the frame is the union of its senses' frames. The query selects among them, and sense attribution is the attention mass per sub-frame.

The context $q_t$ is placed in one of three ways:
- **P0:** none (static learned weights).
- **P1:** a causal mean of the preceding $w=8$ token embeddings. This is the span-channel default.
- **P2:** a mid-network hidden state through a sidecar, in a later experiment (E6).

### 3.2 M2 — factored, ontology-induced mapping

The ontology defines a binary concept × edge-type incidence $M$. A rank-$k$ weighting on its support is

$$W_{i,e} = \langle u_i,\,k_e\rangle\,M_{i,e},$$

where $k_e$ is composed from $r_e$ and $a_e$ (so $K$ never needs $|R\times A|$ rows). The concept factor $u_i$ has three options:
- **(a) free:** a learnable row per training concept. It does not transfer and serves as the memorization upper bound.
- **(b) induced:** $u_i = W_Q\bar c_i$, as in M1. This is the default; it transfers.
- **(c) hybrid:** $u_i = W_Q\bar c_i + \delta_i$ with $\lVert\delta_i\rVert^2$ penalized, and $\delta_j=0$ for unseen $j$.

Weights pass through the mass-preserving softmax of §3.1. The rank is $k \in\{4,8,16,32\}$; E0 and E2 select $k=4$.

### 3.3 M3 — developmental dictionary: a usage-level split test

**Per-usage gradients are free.** Let $z_i = \sum_e w_{i,e} v_e$ and $g_i = \partial L/\partial z_i$, which backpropagation computes anyway. Ignoring the path through the weights, the gradient an atomic vector $a$ receives from concept $i$ through edge $e$ is

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial a}\Big|_i = w_{i,e}\,T_{r_e}^\top g_i,$$

so for `hrr` it is the concept's gradient *unbound* by the relation. A relation vector's per-edge gradient is $J_T(a_e)^\top g_i$. Usage-level gradients therefore cost one FFT per edge, batched with the forward schedule.

**Statistics.** For a dictionary vector $\theta$ with usages $U(\theta)$ (concepts for atomics, edges for relations), we keep:
- usage momenta $m^{(u)} = \mathrm{EMA}_\beta(g^{(u)})$;
- the total momentum $m=\sum_u m^{(u)}$ (Adam's first moment, same $\beta$);
- the usage-agnostic mean absolute gradient $a = \mathrm{EMA}_\beta(|g_t|)$;
- coherence $\kappa = \lVert m\rVert_1/\lVert a\rVert_1$ and activity $\alpha=\lVert a\rVert_1$.

**Screen.** A vector is a candidate if $\alpha$ is high and $\kappa$ low: a vector Adam cannot move despite strong pulls. Coordinate-wise, $\kappa_j\approx|\hat m_j|/\sqrt{\hat v_j}$ is Adam's signal-to-noise ratio, cf. GSNR. The screen alone cannot decide. Under zero-mean i.i.d. noise $\mathbb{E}[\kappa]\approx\sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1+\beta)}$, so a converged vector looks the same as a conflicted one.

**Split gain.** Move along a unit direction $v$ by $\varepsilon$. A shared vector decreases the loss by $\varepsilon|\sum_u\langle m^{(u)},v\rangle|$; two children that split the usages by sign decrease it by $\varepsilon\sum_u|\langle m^{(u)},v\rangle|$. The split gain

$$G(v) = \sum_u\big|\langle m^{(u)},v\rangle\big| - \Big|\sum_u\langle m^{(u)},v\rangle\Big| \;\ge 0$$

is zero iff all usages agree in sign along $v$. Summed over coordinate axes it equals $\lVert\sum_u|m^{(u)}|\rVert_1-\lVert m\rVert_1$, which is the "mean absolute gradient minus momentum" criterion that motivated M3, made exact.

We take $v^*$ as the top eigenvector of the between-usage scatter $S=\sum_u m^{(u)}m^{(u)\top}-\tfrac1{|U|}mm^\top$, and accept a split only if $G^*=G(v^*)$ exceeds the 99th percentile of a **permutation null** (usage labels shuffled over the accumulated per-step contributions, 199 permutations, $p<0.01$).

**Children.** $\theta_\pm=\theta\pm\varepsilon v^*$. Observed usages go to the child matching $\mathrm{sign}\langle m^{(u)},v^*\rangle$, and keys, optimizer state and frames are partitioned. Without the partition and the offset, identical copies would receive identical gradients forever.

**Unobserved usages** (rare, held-out and new concepts) keep the parent vector, which is held at the usage-weighted mean of its children (*parent fallback with synced parents*; chosen after E0, §5.1).

**Budget and phases.** Growth is capped at $\gamma$ of the initial dictionary, with a cooldown after each split. The schedule is warm-up → growth → consolidation (merges only: $\cos>0.98$ and $G_\cup\approx0$) → freeze. Every split and merge is logged as a *card*: parent, children, $v^*$, $G^*$, p-value, usage partition.

### 3.4 M4 — the span channel and the causal injection rule

**Linking.**
- A deterministic longest-match linker over alias tables (WordNet lemmas; MeSH entry terms; ChEBI, taxonomy and EuroVoc labels) runs on **character offsets**, so one linked corpus serves every tokenizer.
- A span is used if it covers at least $\ell_{\min}$ subtokens of the model's tokenizer (default 2). The **span-cardinality table** per tokenizer × $\ell_{\min}$ (linked concepts, occurrences, covered-token fraction, frequency histogram) is computed before any training and decides feasibility (§4.2).
- Held-out concepts and all their aliases are removed from the linker during training. At evaluation they are linked and composed with zero update.

**Injection.**

$$h_t^{(0)} = E[x_t] + p_t + \mathbb{1}[t=e]\;g_t\;P\,c_j(q_t),\qquad g_t=\sigma\big(w_g^\top[E[x_t];\,Pc_j(q_t);\,\mathrm{conf}]+b_g\big),$$

with the gate initialized nearly closed ($b_g=-2$, i.e. $g_0\approx0.12$).

**Prefix-causal last-subtoken rule.** Injecting at the span's *last* subtoken $e$ reveals no future subtoken. However, *deciding* that a linked word ends at $e$ depends on token $e+1$: "bank" vs "banking", "new york" vs "new york city". The implemented linker is therefore prefix-causal. An alias is linked at $e$ if the text up to the end of $e$ ends with it at a word boundary, taking the longest alias ending at $e$ and looking back only. The right boundary is not checked; the gate must learn to discount links like "bank" inside "banking". The injected vector at position $e$ is a function of $x_{\le e}$ only. A property test perturbs every later token and asserts that no input at positions $\le e$ changes. Injecting at the span start or on all span tokens would leak $x_{s+1..e}$ and is excluded for causal LMs (it is fine for encoders such as HRRBERT). The rule is independently supported by mechanistic evidence that LMs assemble multi-token words at their last token (Feucht et al. 2024, 2025; Kaplan et al. 2025).

**Optional semantic head (C7).** $L_{\text{sem}}=\mathrm{InfoNCE}(W_o h_{s-1},\,c_j)$ over the batch's linked concepts predicts a span's meaning before it is spelled out.

**Sparse generation.** Only the concepts linked in a batch are composed (CSR schedule: gather atomics → bind in the FFT domain → segment-softmax → segment-sum). Full-table generation is needed only for tied-output and compression variants (§5.6).

**Parameter accounting.**
- The channel adds $(|A|+|R|)\,d + \sum_r \mathrm{params}(T_r) + 3dd_k + dd_m$ + gate.
- A free per-concept table adds $|C_{\text{train}}|\,d_m$.

Global, relation, atomic, concept-local and host-adapter groups are reported separately.

### 3.5 M5 — self-authored ontologies (pretrained hosts)

A pretrained host can author frames for concepts its ontology lacks. Each round $r = 0, 1, \ldots$ has six steps:
1. **Discover.** Find spans with $\ell\ge\ell_{\min}$ that are frequent in the reading corpus but unlinked, ranked by excess surprisal $\Delta_j$ over a frequency-matched baseline.
2. **Author.** The host proposes edges $(r, a)$ under a constrained template (closed relation vocabulary by default; $K$ samples per authoring context).
3. **Verify.** Keep an edge only if it is self-consistent across samples and its **held-out utility** on validation contexts disjoint from the authoring contexts has a positive bootstrap lower bound. For an existing frame, $U_{j,e}=-\sum_{\text{occ. of }j}\langle g_j, v_e\rangle$ is the first-order loss change of adding $e$ with a small mass, a TracIn/LESS-style inner product scored from one backward pass. Brand-new concepts (empty frames) use the exact loss difference with and without the edge instead.
4. **Integrate.** The frames enter the linker as provisional; new fillers are allocated through M3.
5. **Train.** Train the channel (and optionally a LoRA) with general-text replay. Real data are accumulated, not replaced.
6. **Audit.** Measure locality, precision and recall against hidden gold edges, and drift. Stop when the median utility of new edges is ≤ 0 or locality fails.

Gold-audit edges never enter acceptance. Every accepted edge is logged as an authoring card.

### 3.6 Objective

$$L = L_{\mathrm{LM}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{sem}}L_{\mathrm{sem}} + \lambda_\delta\textstyle\sum_i\lVert\delta_i\rVert^2 + \lambda_\tau R_\tau$$

plus the growth budget of §3.3. Two regimes are used:
- **Joint (from scratch).** The transformer and the channel train together. This is the pre-registered core.
- **Continued pretraining.** The pretrained host is frozen or LoRA-adapted, and the channel is trained. This is the regime in which M5 is possible.

### 4.5 Strata, metrics and statistics

**Strata.** Every LM metric is reported for:
- all tokens;
- unlinked tokens (locality);
- tokens inside linked spans;
- the 8 tokens *after* a span, split by the concept's training frequency: held-out (zero bin); unseen-not-held-out (`after_unseen`) and seen-rare 1–9 (`after_rare_seen`), split this way after decision 16; mid 10–99; frequent ≥ 100;
- span length in subtokens: 1, 2, 3+.

**Probes.** Each probe is run in prompting and linear-probe form on final-layer states:
- WiC;
- all-words WSD (ALL);
- rare-word similarity (CARD-660, RW);
- hypernymy (BLESS interaction probe as primary, decision 9; HyperLex);
- LAMBADA for retention.

**Statistics.**
- Seeds: ≥ 3 for every candidate and control.
- Final loss per stratum: mean over seeds with a seed-level t interval.
- Paired differences (condition − reference): token-weighted, with a cluster bootstrap over evaluation windows (each window carries every seed; 10,000 resamples).
- Multiple comparisons: Holm correction over the condition grid within each stratum; "significant" means Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.
- Locality: one-sided (upper 95% bound of the relative unlinked-loss change ≤ 0.5%).
- "Matches C2 on seen-rare": non-inferiority with a 0.5% margin.
- One pre-registered primary endpoint per experiment; all attempted budgets are reported.
- Pre-registration, control sizes, holdout hash, measured throughput and the escalation rule are frozen at G2 (`experiments/e4-small-lm/preregistration.md`) `[[G2]]`. Later changes are logged as deviations.
- E0 and E2 report 95% Student-t intervals over 3 seeds, paired by seed.

### 4.6 Feasibility from span cardinality

A track's gate is powered on its evaluation split if it has:
- ≥ 300 held-out entries with ≥ 5 occurrences and ≥ 2,000 held-out span occurrences;
- likewise for the seen-rare stratum.

With a per-occurrence paired loss difference of SD ≈ 0.5–1 nat, 2,000 occurrences give SE ≈ 0.011–0.022 nat, a minimum detectable difference of ≈ 0.04–0.08 nat at 80% power after Holm over ≈ 4 comparisons. The C7 tracks used a lower bar (≥ 1,000 spans, ≥ 50 entries). The two are harmonized at G2: the T1 bar governs gate claims, and the lower bar marks a track "exploratory-feasible" (decision 17).

### 4.7 Convergence speed and escalation

Committed runs are short. Their purpose is to measure how fast each condition learns. Every run evaluates its fixed evaluation set by stratum at log-spaced checkpoints (10M, 20M, 40M, … tokens). From these we compute:
- the **data multiplier** $k(L)=D_{C0}(L)/D_{\text{cond}}(L)$, the ratio of tokens needed to reach loss $L$ on a stratum (CI over seeds);
- fitted curves $L(D)=E+B\,D^{-\beta}$ per condition × stratum, with bootstrapped projections of the gap at 2.5B and 7B tokens;
- the compute-adjusted multiplier (FLOPs and wall clock, including channel overhead);
- the size trend of $k$ (50M → 125M; 135M → 360M → 0.5B for hosts).

**Escalation rule.** A question moves to a larger tier only if all three hold at the largest committed budget:
1. the lower CI bound of $k$ on its target stratum exceeds 1.1;
2. the gain is not explained by C1, C2 or C1h;
3. either the projected gap has a CI excluding zero, or $k$ does not shrink with model size.

Nothing beyond the committed ≈ 400 GPU-hours runs without that evidence and the author's approval.

For frozen hosts, C0′ has a flat curve, so $k(L)$ is undefined. Their results are paired loss gaps at fixed budgets (decision 4).

### 4.8 LLM-graded evaluations (estimates)

Human judgement (explanation quality, neighbours of rare concepts, discovered senses, authored edges) is estimated by an LLM judge, labelled as an LLM-graded estimate in every claim. The same items, rubric and pre-registered $n$ are kept for a later clinician and expert study. The protocol:
- Judge: Claude, model pinned to `claude-opus-5-5`.
- Each item is graded in 3 independent calls cycling over 2 prompt paraphrases, blinded, with randomized order and position.
- The rubric is fixed before grading.
- Verdicts are cached with model ID and prompt hash.

**Calibration (C5).** 30 ontology-gold items (WordNet hypernym-related vs random concept–neighbour lists, 0–3 relatedness):
- v1, headless CLI: accuracy 30/30, Fleiss κ across calls 0.812;
- v2, Claude Code subagents: 30/30, κ 0.926.

Both pass the pre-set bar (accuracy ≥ 0.9, κ ≥ 0.6). Agreement numbers are comparable across harnesses; individual verdicts are not. Each study records its harness.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 E0 — synthetic identifiability (final; gate G1 passed)

**Setup.**
- CPU, single-threaded, 3 seeds (101, 202, 303), $d=64$ (`tables/e0.md`, Fig. `figures/e0_identifiability`).
- **D0.1:** hidden dictionaries (64 atomics, 6 relations); 400 concepts of degree 3–8; 8 context prototypes, 2 held out; teacher weights $w^*_{i,e}(q)=|F_i|\,\mathrm{softmax}\langle W^*q, k^*_e\rangle$ with context strength 0.5–4, plus a static teacher. Learners: M0, 01b feature salience, M1 without query (P0), M1 with query, and free per-(concept, context) weights. Evaluation is on held-out concepts × held-out contexts.
- **D0.3:** a rank-$k^*=4$ bilinear teacher; learners use free, induced or hybrid concept factors at $k\in\{4,8,16,32\}$; composition-disjoint held-out concepts.
- **D0.2:** collapsed students. One condition has 48 atomics of which 8 are polysemous (two hidden senses used by disjoint concept subsets); the other has 6 relations of which 2 hide two sub-operators. 600 concepts, 100 held out. Schedule: 1,500 warm-up, 2,000 growth and 1,000 final steps. $\beta=0.9$, screen every 200 steps, 199 permutations, $p<0.01$, merge cosine 0.98.

**D0.1 — a context query recovers context-dependent composition, and does no harm without it.**
- Static bundle (M0) held-out cosine: falls from 0.596 to 0.484 as context strength rises from 0.5 to 4.
- M1 with the context query: stays at 0.945–0.968.
- Paired gain M1(q) − M0: +0.359 [+0.336, +0.382], +0.394 [+0.308, +0.480], +0.433 [+0.228, +0.638], +0.460 [+0.124, +0.797] at strengths 0.5, 1, 2, 4.
- Static teacher: −0.000014 [−0.000023, −0.000004], inside the 0.005 no-harm margin.
- M1 without a query degrades with context strength (0.884 → 0.560), as it must.
- Free per-(concept, context) weights, which cannot transfer to held-out contexts, reach only 0.664–0.802.

**D0.3 — an induced concept factor transfers.**
- Induced factor at $k=k^*=4$: held-out cosine 0.976 [0.939, 1.013]; hybrid 0.978.
- Free factor: 0.598–0.696; static bundle: 0.623.
- Larger ranks lose a little (induced 0.893 at $k=32$). We carry $k=4$ forward.

**D0.2 — the split test recovers planted polysemy; consolidation is required.** In the development run, M3 with the permutation null reaches precision = recall = 1.0 and ARI 1.0 on training usages, for both atomics and relation sub-types. No false split survives consolidation. Three findings qualify this:
- *The screen alone over-splits.* Before consolidation it splits 37.5–40% of the *non*-polysemous vectors (raw false-split rate). Merging is therefore a required phase, not an option.
- *The alternatives are worse.* The Anderson–Darling variant keeps more false splits (0.083 in development, 0.142 with the default). Coherence-only screening misses senses (recall 0.875–0.917 for atomics, 0.667 for relations). Random splits at matched count do not recover structure (ARI 0.018).
- *Held-out usages.* Usages without gradient evidence were routed near chance (held-out ARI ≈ 0.05). In the development run, splitting therefore *lowered* held-out composition for relations: −0.063 [−0.099, −0.027] cosine vs no growth.

With the M3 default adopted after this run (unobserved usages keep the parent, which tracks the usage-weighted mean of its children):
- atomics: precision 0.963 (seed range 0.889–1.000), recall 1.0, ARI 1.0, surviving false-split rate 0.008;
- relations: 1.0 / 1.0 / 1.0 / 0;
- held-out composition no longer degrades: atomics +0.0041 [+0.0002, +0.0081]; relations +0.0071 [−0.0021, +0.0163] vs no growth.

**Gate G1: PASS.** All three mechanisms recover planted structure. Every committed E0 number reproduces bit-for-bit on a second machine (8,100 + 154 cells at 1e-12).

### 5.3 E2 — mapping × operator frontier on frozen anchors (final)

**Setup.**
- Concepts: 6,000 WordNet synsets aligned to single GPT-2 tokens, and 6,000 MeSH 2026 descriptors with scope notes.
- Each target is a frozen GPT-2 anchor: the final-layer state at the term's last subtoken after `"Definition: {gloss} Term: {term}"`, centred on the training mean and normalized.

**Absolute fit is poor.** Test MRR is 0.033–0.040 (WordNet) and 0.067–0.076 (MeSH) across all 49 cells, and variance explained is negative everywhere. This repeats the 01c finding that frozen GPT-2 space is a poor reconstruction target. E2's endpoints are therefore relative.

- Hybrid δ adds nothing on held-out concepts (+0.0003 [−0.0017, +0.0023]; +0.0011 [−0.0068, +0.0090]), as expected for a concept-local residual.

- selected − `random_fixed`: MeSH −0.0001 [−0.0054, +0.0052]; WordNet −0.0015 [−0.0094, +0.0064];

### 5.6 Cost of the channel (final for generation; training overhead pending)

Composing only the concepts linked in a batch is what makes the channel affordable (Fig. `figures/generation_sparse_vs_full`; `tables/generation.md`).

CPU setting: composer width 256, 8,192 atomics, mean degree 6, 1,024 Zipf-distributed linked spans per batch.
- A training step on the sparse path costs 28–46 ms and 40–57 MiB of extra memory for tables of 10k to 100k concepts. Memory does not depend on table size.
- Materializing the whole table every step costs 474 ms → 4.7 s and 0.47 → 5.5 GiB as N grows from 10k to 100k.
- At N = 100k the sparse path is 164× faster and uses 118× less memory for the static bundle; 116× and 96× for the attentive mapping.
- On a 1.5 GiB GPU allocation, the full path no longer fits from N = 50k, while the sparse step needs 41–58 MiB at N = 100k.

## 6. Limitations

- **Open-clinical substitute.** SNOMED CT, UMLS and MIMIC-IV were not available, so the clinical track uses MeSH 2026 and PubMed abstracts and is labelled "open-clinical". No clinical-coding (ICD) result is reported; that task is marked "not run". MeSH frames (tree positions, pharmacological actions, see-also) are shallower than SNOMED CT's defining relations, so T1 likely understates what a richer clinical ontology could provide. PubMed text may overlap with what the pretrained hosts saw, although we used the newest 2025–26 abstracts. Zero-shot claims on pretrained hosts rest only on the synthetic private concepts.
- **LLM-graded estimates.** Explanation quality, rare-concept neighbours, discovered senses and authored edges are rated by a calibrated LLM judge (30/30 on ontology-gold items; κ 0.81–0.93). They are reported as **estimates**, not as expert judgements. No clinical claim is made before a clinician study on the identical items.
- **Single-GPU budget.** Every committed run fits ≈ 400 GPU-hours on one RTX 3090: 50M and 125M models at ≤ 500M tokens, hosts up to 0.5B parameters at 100M tokens. Early-training advantages can wash out, so we report fitted curves and projected gaps with CIs and say when a gain is early-only. Larger runs are escalation tiers decided by pre-registered evidence and are not part of this paper unless approved `[[R8]]`.
- **Frozen-host refutation.** The original frozen-host approach (fit VSA composition to a pretrained model's rows) failed, and our first recorded positive (a low-rank transfer) was a leakage artefact found by an audit. We report this rather than omit it. It also limits what the frozen-anchor experiments (E1, E2) can show: they measure relative structure, not usable row insertion.
- **Operator not yet separated.** On frozen anchors, binding operators do not beat a random fixed operator. Unless the joint-regime ablation separates them, our results concern compositional parameter sharing over ontology edges, not the algebra of binding.
- **Flat frames and linker limits.** Frames are one-hop relation–filler sets: no paths, negation, time or cardinality. Superposition capacity limits high-degree frames, which are capped at 16 edges. The prefix-causal linker over-links prefixes ("bank" in "banking") and under-links polysemy, and the gate must discount bad links. Linking is deterministic and not learned.
- **Feasibility limits.** Two of the five built application tracks (product, legal) cannot power a seen-rare stratum at 100M tokens. Their results cover only held-out concepts and locality.
- **Lookup-memory control.** Our hashed span memory (C1h) matches parameters, site and gate but is simpler than the strongest 2026 n-gram memories. Unless an Engram-faithful variant is added, "structure beats lookup" is claimed only at matched parameters, injection site and gate `[[G2]]`.
- **Statistics.** E0 and E2 use 3 seeds and t intervals, so several CIs are wide (e.g. D0.1 at the highest context strength). Rates such as precision and false-split rate are reported with their seed range.

## 7. Reproducibility

Summary; details in [reproducibility.md](reproducibility.md).

- **Code and runs.** Every run writes `resolved_config.yaml`, `manifest.json` (git SHA, dirty flag, packages, device, threads, argv, config hash), metrics and a report. Runs are committed before results are cited. Dirty-tree manifests are disclosed.
- **Data.** All sources were downloaded on 2026-10-02 and hashed twice against the publishers' checksums. Every held-out list is frozen and hashed before any run that could see it.
- **Cross-machine reproduction.** The original machine had faulty RAM. Every committed result was re-run on a second machine:
  - single-threaded CPU experiments match bit-for-bit (E0: 8,254 cells at 1e-12);
  - GPU memory and PTQ perplexities match exactly;
  - throughput matches within 1% and probe scores within 0.0035.
- **Figures and tables.** All are regenerated deterministically from committed run folders by `manuscript/figures/make_figures.py`. Input hashes and run commits are recorded in `figures/sources.json`.
- **Compute.** Compute is accounted per block in a ledger (planned ≈ 400 GPU-hours; measured values filled from the job-queue logs).
