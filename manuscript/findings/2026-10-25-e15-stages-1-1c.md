# E15, "HRR as a training target": stages 1, 1b and 1c (2026-10-22 to 25)

**Recorded:** 2026-10-25 by workplace-8e. **Sources:** `experiments/e15-hrr-target/report/{t5, t5-stage1b, t5-stage1c}/report.md`.
**Pre-registration:** `experiments/e15-hrr-target/preregistration.md` (stage 1; §16 stage 1b incl. the C5-long control;
§17 stage 1c; §18 stage 1d, registered 2026-10-25 and queued, not yet run). **Host:** SmolLM2-360M, full fine-tuning,
E9's T5 recipe. **Conventions:** property accuracy on the 285 compositional held-out new words (every atom seen in
training, the combination new) unless stated; 3 seeds pooled; paired bootstrap; \* = Holm-significant in the
registered family; secondaries unadjusted. Chance for read accuracy over 3,493 atoms is 0.0003.

## 1. Why E15 exists

Across E4, E9 and the binding programme the composed HRR vector was only an input feature: no loss ever touched its
structure from the model's side, and the model learned to treat it as a per-term identifier (C5 ≈ C5sh ≈ C2w; readout
arms null; implicit route ≈ 0 on real text). E15 adds losses on the vector itself: **write** (the layer-11 hidden state
must reproduce the term's bundle through a linear map W) and **read** (each filler must be recoverable from W·h by
unbinding with its role and cleanup against the atom table). Stage 1c adds objectives in token space instead.

## 4. Reading

1. **The algebra is learnable by direct supervision, slowly.** Read accuracy on never-seen combinations rises from
   chance to 0.76 over 200M tokens and is still rising. Every free-table control stays at 0.02–0.05: the structure,
   not memorisation, carries it. No earlier arm in the programme got a model to read a role at all.
2. **Reading does not become use.** Implicit QA on the same words reaches 0.375 at 200M, of which 0.065 is length
   alone and 0.046 the objective; the same facts as text give 0.82. The decode–use gap is the result.
3. **Token-space objectives do not fix it at 360M.** Distilling from the explicit route harms the LM and the implicit
   route alike; verbalising facts helps the LM and barely touches the implicit route. The only thing that moves
   vector reading is supervising the vector, and that moves use a little.
4. **Behaviour follows content when the model is forced to carry the vector** (C5+WR −1.9% vs C5sh+WR +8.7% at
   50M), the first such result in the programme — but at matched length the LM benefit shrinks to −0.27% (n.s.).
5. **Mechanism:** unseen-filler atoms decode as well as seen ones, so what the model learns is to carry the injected
   vector to layer 11 nearly intact; the atom table does the rest. This is "preserve and unbind", not a learned
   per-relation decoder.

## 6. What stops and what continues

- **Stops:** route distillation and fact verbalisation as routes to implicit use; the higher read weight.
- **Continues:** stage 1d (500M, one seed each; predictions registered: read 0.80–0.83, objective's QA share
  +0.04–0.06, i.e. no change of conclusion); the loss-domain finding goes to the claims ledger with its matched-length
  caveat.
- **For the manuscript:** the strong hypothesis — a model learns composed facts through language modelling — is now
  closed in every form tried (side effect, readout head, direct vector supervision, aligned token-space objectives).
  The narrow positive is new and specific: a 360M host can be trained to decode an HRR vector it has never seen
  (0.76 of facts, structure-dependent), and that decoding does not translate into use through the weights at this
  scale, while the same facts through the prompt give 0.82.
