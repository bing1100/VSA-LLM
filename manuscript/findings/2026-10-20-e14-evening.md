## 5. Tier-F CPU studies run tonight (pre-registration §19; designs written before they ran)

### X8b: irregular trees
**Source:** `experiments/e14-logic/runs/lga-concat-x8b/`. Three tree families (Galton–Watson growth, deep-sparse,
spine-shaped), depths 8–32, 3 seeds.

**Results:**
- **The pre-order layout with subtree sizes (`preorder-concat`) is exact (1.000) in every cell,** at widths 2,513 to
  87,983. The heap layout is also exact, but its nominal width reaches 5.5 × 10¹¹ at depth 32: at least 600× wider.
- **At 256-dimensional blocks** every block layout is exact, up to 687 internal nodes.
- **At 64-dimensional blocks** node memory degrades with tree size (0.945 → 0.365 on Galton–Watson trees, depth
  8 → 32). The hybrid (heap for the top levels, node memory below) is better in every cell (0.975 → 0.609).
- **One nested vector fails** on bushy trees (0.11–0.33). It reaches 0.39–0.63 only on spine trees, which have d
  internal nodes.

**Reading:** for irregular trees, lay them out in pre-order with subtree sizes.

### LG-P3b: premise retrieval at scale
**Source:** `experiments/e14-logic/runs/lgp3b-retrieval/`. Up to 10⁵ clauses; independent, shared-stem or
near-duplicate class names; widths 1,024–8,192; exact queries and one-typo queries.

**Results:**
- **Exact queries:** recall@5 is 1.000 in every cell, including 10⁵ clauses with near-duplicate names (cosine 0.81).
  R-precision is also 1.000. The predictions that near-duplicates or R-precision would fail first were not met.
- **One-typo queries:** exact match scores 0.00. VSA codes built from character trigrams score 0.99–1.00, and a
  one-edit expanded symbol index scores 0.997–1.000.

**Reading:** the store ties exact symbol retrieval and never beats it. On typos it only ties a cheap edit-expansion
index.

## 6. A smoke observation that changes X7's reading (to be confirmed by the queued X7b run)

**The observation.** While building X7b, a 4-step CPU smoke on SmolLM2-135M showed:
- an *untrained* 768-wide composer decodes T4 slots at 0.998;
- an untrained 256-wide composer decodes at 0.927;
- 256-wide training costs −0.022.

So X7's ensemble gain (0.979 against 0.89–0.92 for one store) is most likely a **width** effect, present at
initialisation, not knowledge that three trainings combine.

**What follows:**
- §19 added untrained `init-256` and `init-768` controls before anything was queued.
- The queued X7b training run tests whether 768-wide *training* keeps that decoding.
- Until then, treat X7's "the ensemble removes decode noise" as width, not as an ensemble benefit.

## 7. Reproducibility caveat for the tier-P item files

**The problem.** `logic.store.Codebook` seeds its vectors from Python's per-process `hash()`. Rebuilding the tier-P LG
item files gives identical items, questions and gold, but some VSA contexts differ:
- the confidence numbers;
- some reads, e.g. `path-vsa` text in 162 of 1,100 tree items.

**Status:**
- The files on disk are the record of what was scored.
- Their sha256 hashes are unchanged by tonight's work.
- New item sets (LG-D2's `trees-noisy`) use a SHA-256-seeded codebook and rebuild byte-identically.
- A fix for the old builders would change their output, so it waits for a decision.
